  SECTION 02  -  WHAT THIS SOFTWARE DOES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
===============================================================================

  WHAT IT DOES
  ------------

  Taurus Vision reads the trading history and the live account state of the
  MetaTrader 5 account that is currently logged in, and turns it into a full
  screen analytics dashboard drawn directly on the chart. It gives you, in one
  place, the kind of account report that traders normally have to upload to an
  external website to see.

  It answers questions such as:

    -  How much has this account really grown, after deposits and withdrawals
       are taken out of the picture?
    -  What is the deepest fall this account has ever taken from its own peak?
    -  Which currency pairs actually make the money, and which ones only make
       the trade count look busy?
    -  Are the long trades better than the short trades?
    -  Which day of the week and which hour of the day are profitable, and
       which ones quietly eat the account?
    -  How long is a trade held on average, and does that differ between buy
       and sell trades?
    -  Is the win rate supported by the size of the wins, or is it a large
       number of small wins hiding a few very large losses?

  It also lets you close any open position from the dashboard itself, with a
  deliberate two step confirmation so that nothing closes by accident.

  WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
  -------------------

  Please read this list carefully and explain it to every student.

    -  It is NOT a trading signal system. It never tells you to buy or sell.
    -  It NEVER opens a trade. There is no entry logic inside this software.
    -  It NEVER modifies a Stop Loss or a Take Profit.
    -  It NEVER places a pending order.
    -  It does NOT send your data anywhere. It has no internet connection,
       no web request, no telemetry and no licence server. Everything it
       shows is read from your own terminal, on your own computer.
    -  It does NOT predict the future. Every number on the screen describes
       what has already happened.

  The ONLY trading action this software can perform is closing a position that
  you have selected and confirmed twice on screen. Section 13 explains that
  feature completely.

===============================================================================
  SECTION 04  -  INSTALLATION, STEP BY STEP
===============================================================================

  Follow these steps exactly. They take about two minutes.

  STEP 1  -  Open the data folder
  --------------------------------
  In MetaTrader 5, click on the menu:

      File  >  Open Data Folder

  A Windows folder opens. This is the private folder of your terminal. Inside
  it you will see a folder called MQL5.

  STEP 2  -  Place the file
  -------------------------
  Open:

      MQL5  >  Experts

  Copy the file

      ForexGuru_Pk_Taurus_Vision_Trader_Dashboard.mq5

  into that Experts folder.

  IMPORTANT. It goes into "Experts", NOT into "Indicators". Version 9.00 is an
  Expert Advisor. If you place it in the Indicators folder it will either fail
  to appear or fail to close trades. Section 16 explains why this had to
  change.

  STEP 3  -  Compile
  ------------------
  Back in MetaTrader 5, press F4, or click:

      Tools  >  MetaQuotes Language Editor

  MetaEditor opens. In the Navigator on the left, open:

      Experts  >  ForexGuru_Pk_Taurus_Vision_Trader_Dashboard.mq5

  Press F7, or click the "Compile" button.

  You should see, in the Errors tab at the bottom:

      0 error(s), 0 warning(s)

  A file with the extension .ex5 is now created beside the .mq5 file. That
  .ex5 file is the program the terminal actually runs.

  STEP 4  -  Refresh the Navigator
  --------------------------------
  Return to MetaTrader 5. In the Navigator window (Ctrl+N), right click on
  "Expert Advisors" and choose "Refresh". The entry

      ForexGuru_Pk_Taurus_Vision_Trader_Dashboard

  now appears in the list.

  STEP 5  -  Attach it to a chart
  -------------------------------
  Open any chart. The symbol and the timeframe do not matter at all - the
  dashboard studies the whole ACCOUNT, not the chart it sits on. Many traders
  keep one dedicated chart, for example EURUSD on the Daily timeframe, purely
  as the dashboard chart.

  Drag the Expert Advisor from the Navigator onto that chart. The settings
  window opens.

  STEP 6  -  Tick "Allow Algo Trading"
  ------------------------------------
  In the settings window, open the "Common" tab and tick:

      [x] Allow Algo Trading

  Do this even if you do not intend to use the close buttons. It costs
  nothing, and it avoids a confusing error later.

  STEP 7  -  Type your name, then press OK
  ----------------------------------------
  Open the "Inputs" tab. Find the very first field under the TRADER heading:

      >>> TYPE YOUR FULL NAME HERE <<<

  Replace the placeholder text "Enter Your Name Here" with your own full name.
  Press OK.

  STEP 8  -  Switch on AutoTrading
  --------------------------------
  On the main toolbar of the terminal, the "AutoTrading" button must be green.
  If it is red, click it once.

  Installation is complete. The dashboard fills the chart immediately.

===============================================================================
  SECTION 06  -  ANATOMY OF THE SCREEN
===============================================================================

  From top to bottom, the dashboard is built from five fixed zones. The middle
  zone changes with the tab you select; everything else stays where it is.

  ZONE 1  -  THE HEADER
  ---------------------
  A dark gradient bar with a thin gold line under it. It carries three blocks:

    LEFT     Broker company name
             Server: <trade server name>
             Leverage: 1:<leverage>  |  <account currency>

    CENTRE   "Trading Dashboard Of <Your Name>"  in large gold letters
             Underneath: the product name and the version.
             Before you set your name, the centre shows the product name and
             a red instruction line instead.

    RIGHT    Account: <account login number>
             Balance: <current balance>
             Equity:  <current equity>

  The centre title carries no brackets around the trader name. That is
  deliberate and part of the brand style.

  ZONE 2  -  THE TOOLBAR
  ----------------------
  A single row of controls under the header.

    LEFT     Five tab chips:  Overview | Statistics | Analytics | History |
             About. The active tab is filled with gold.

    RIGHT    The theme switch, showing a drawn moon and the word "Dark", or a
             drawn sun and the word "Light".
             The chips "Expand All" and "Collapse All".
             A quiet text line: "Updated hh:mm:ss".

    The sun and the moon are drawn with shapes, not with a font character.
    That is intentional: a symbol font may be missing on a student computer,
    and a missing font character would render as an empty box.

    When the chart is narrow, the toolbar drops controls from the right hand
    side rather than overlapping them. The tabs are never dropped.

  ZONE 3  -  THE KPI RIBBON
  -------------------------
  A row of up to seven cards giving the headline numbers of the account. Each
  card has a small coloured stripe on its left edge, a label, a large value
  and a small note in the bottom right corner.

    1. GAIN (TIME WEIGHTED)   note: absolute gain
    2. BALANCE                note: peak balance ever reached
    3. EQUITY                 note: account leverage
    4. FLOATING P/L           note: number of open positions
    5. CLOSED PROFIT          note: number of closed trades
    6. MAX DRAWDOWN           note: the same drawdown in money
    7. WIN RATE               note: profit factor

  If the chart is too narrow to give every card a readable width, the ribbon
  drops cards from the right, down to a minimum of three. It never squeezes
  seven unreadable cards into the space.

  The ribbon can be switched off entirely with the setting Show Top KPI
  Ribbon. It is also hidden automatically on the About tab, because the About
  tab is a product page and not an account report.

  ZONE 4  -  THE PANEL BODY
  -------------------------
  This is the working area. Its contents change with the selected tab. Every
  item in it is a "panel": a rounded rectangle with a title bar, an optional
  note on the right hand side of the title bar, and a body.

  The layout engine gives each row of panels the height it asks for. When
  there is spare height it hands the surplus to the panels that gain the most
  from being taller - a chart gains, a six row table does not. When there is
  not enough height it takes the height back from the panels that survive it
  best, and only then squeezes everything proportionally.

  This is why the dashboard looks correct on a small laptop screen and on a
  large monitor without any manual adjustment.

  ZONE 5  -  THE FOOTER
  ---------------------
  A dark gradient bar with a gold line above it:

    LEFT     Your name  |  account currency  |  account number
    CENTRE   Version  |  phone number  |  support email
    RIGHT    "Powered By ForexGuru.Pk"

  The footer is designed to survive a screenshot. When a student sends you a
  picture of the dashboard, the footer already tells you whose account it is,
  in which currency, and on which account number.


===============================================================================
  SECTION 07  -  HOW TO OPERATE THE DASHBOARD (EVERY CONTROL EXPLAINED)
===============================================================================

  Everything in the dashboard is operated with a single LEFT mouse click on
  the chart. There is no right click menu, no keyboard shortcut and no drag
  operation.

  7.1  SWITCHING TABS
  -------------------
  Click one of the five chips on the left of the toolbar. The panel body is
  replaced immediately. The tab you were on is remembered only for as long as
  the dashboard runs; when it is restarted it returns to the tab named in the
  setting "Opening Tab".

  7.2  COLLAPSING AND EXPANDING A PANEL
  -------------------------------------
  Click anywhere on the TITLE BAR of a panel. The panel folds up to just its
  title bar, and the panels around it take the freed space. Click the title
  bar again to expand it.

  This is the fastest way to give one chart the whole screen. For example, on
  the Overview tab, collapse "Account Summary" and "Current Active Trades",
  and the Account Growth chart expands to almost the full window - perfect for
  a screenshot in class.

  7.3  EXPAND ALL / COLLAPSE ALL
  ------------------------------
  These two chips in the toolbar act on every panel in the whole product at
  once, on all five tabs, not only on the tab you are looking at. Use them to
  reset the layout after experimenting.

  7.4  THE THEME SWITCH
  ---------------------
  Click the chip showing the moon or the sun.

    Dark  (Luxury Gold)      - gold on near black. The normal working theme.
    Light (Print/Screenshot) - dark ink on white. Made for printing, for
                               projecting on a classroom screen, and for
                               screenshots that will be pasted into a white
                               document or a PDF.

  The glyph shown is the theme that is currently live: a moon for Dark, a sun
  for Light.

  Changing the theme also re-applies the chart background colour, so the chart
  behind the dashboard matches instantly.

  7.5  THE SHARED PAGER
  ---------------------
  Six tables in this product can hold more rows than fit on the screen:

      -  Current Active Trades
      -  Closed Trade History
      -  Deposits & Withdrawals
      -  Reward : Risk By Symbol
      -  Average Holding Time
      -  Currency Popularity

  All six use the SAME pager control, on the right hand side of the panel
  title bar:

      <     Page 2 / 5   -   137 Closed     >

  Click ">" for the next page and "<" for the previous page.

  Two behaviours are worth teaching:

    (a) Both arrows WRAP. From the last page, ">" returns to page 1. From
        page 1, "<" jumps to the last page.

    (b) On a narrow panel there may be no room to draw the "<" arrow. The
        ">" arrow is always drawn. Because ">" wraps, you can always reach
        every page and get back to the start, even when the back arrow is
        not visible.

  The pager also shows a note when there is only ONE page - for example
  "3 Open Positions" or "Net $2,500.00". In that case no arrows are drawn,
  because there is nowhere to go.

  Each table remembers its own page independently. Moving to page 3 of the
  Closed Trade History does not disturb the Reward : Risk table.

  7.6  IN-PANEL TAB STRIPS
  ------------------------
  Two panels carry a small strip of chips inside their body:

    Account Growth ....... Growth % | Balance | Profit | Drawdown
    Currency Popularity .. By Symbol | By Strategy

  Click a chip to change what the panel displays. The active chip is filled
  with gold.

  7.7  THE YEAR STRIP
  -------------------
  The "Monthly Gain (Change)" panel carries one chip per year in which the
  account traded. Click a year to study that year. The dashboard opens on the
  NEWEST year by default.

  7.8  THE CLOSE BUTTONS
  ----------------------
  A small red "X" appears at the right hand end of each row in the Current
  Active Trades table. This is the only control in the product that can change
  your account. It is explained fully in Section 13.

  7.9  HOW OFTEN THE SCREEN UPDATES
  ---------------------------------
  Three separate clocks keep the dashboard alive:

    -  A one second timer redraws the screen once per second, so the numbers
       keep moving even on a quiet market with no incoming ticks.
    -  Incoming price ticks trigger a redraw, but no more often than about
       four times per second, so a fast market cannot flood the terminal.
    -  The full history recalculation - the expensive part - runs only every
       few seconds, controlled by the setting "History Recalculation Interval
       (Sec)", default 5.

  When you open or close a trade, the terminal tells the dashboard, and the
  history is rebuilt on the next timer beat, about one second later. The
  rebuild is deliberately NOT done inside the trade notification itself,
  because that would block the terminal's trading pipeline and freeze the
  platform for a moment on every order.

  7.10  A CLICK GUARD YOU MAY NOTICE
  ----------------------------------
  Two clicks on the SAME control inside 300 milliseconds are treated as one.
  This stops an accidental double click from flipping the theme twice or
  skipping two pages at once. If you want to move two pages, click, wait a
  moment, then click again.


===============================================================================
  SECTION 08  -  TAB 1  -  OVERVIEW
===============================================================================

  The daily working view. Three panels: what the account is, how it grew, and
  what is open right now.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  8.1  PANEL: ACCOUNT SUMMARY
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Title bar note: the date of the first record found in the account history.

  Twelve values in two columns.

    LEFT COLUMN

    Abs. Gain ......... Absolute gain. Total closed profit divided by total
                        deposits, as a percentage. This answers: "For every
                        100 rupees or dollars I put in, how much has trading
                        added?"

    Daily ............. The average COMPOUNDED daily return implied by the
                        whole growth of the account, spread evenly over the
                        number of days between the first and the last record.
                        It is not the return of today.

    Monthly ........... The same figure compounded over thirty days.

    Deposits .......... Sum of every deposit found in the history.

    Withdrawals ....... Sum of every withdrawal, shown as a positive number.

    Interest/Swap ..... Total swap paid or received across all closed trades.
                        Green when the account received swap overall, red when
                        it paid.

    RIGHT COLUMN

    Highest Bal. ...... The highest balance the account has ever reached.

    Max Drawdown ...... The deepest fall, in percent, from a peak balance to
                        the lowest point after that peak.

    Closed Profit ..... Net profit of all closed trading, including commission
                        and swap. It excludes deposits and withdrawals.

    Free Margin ....... Live value from the terminal.

    Used Margin ....... Live value from the terminal.

    Margin Level ...... Live value from the terminal, as a percentage. Shows
                        a dash when no position is open, because the figure is
                        undefined with zero used margin.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  8.2  PANEL: ACCOUNT GROWTH
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Title bar note: the date of the first point and the date of the last point.

  The main chart of the product. A tab strip inside the panel selects one of
  four views of the same account.

    GROWTH %      Time weighted growth. This is the fair way to measure a
                  trading account that has received deposits and paid out
                  withdrawals. A deposit of one lakh does not make the line
                  jump upward, because a deposit is not a trading result.
                  Only trading moves this line.

                  In this view only, a second, quieter grey line is drawn on
                  top: the raw BALANCE. Comparing the two lines is one of the
                  most useful teaching pictures in the whole dashboard. When
                  the grey balance line rises but the gold growth line is
                  flat, the account is growing by deposits, not by trading.

    BALANCE       The raw account balance after every deal.

    PROFIT        Cumulative trading profit in money, starting from zero.

    DRAWDOWN      The drawdown percentage at each point, drawn downward from
                  zero, in red. This shows not only how deep the falls were,
                  but how LONG the account stayed under water.

  On every view:

    -  A green dot on the bottom axis marks a DEPOSIT.
    -  A red dot on the bottom axis marks a WITHDRAWAL.
    -  Five horizontal grid lines carry the value axis on the left.
    -  The zero line, when it is inside the visible range, is drawn slightly
       brighter than the grid.
    -  The first and last dates are printed under the two ends of the chart.
    -  A legend under the chart names every line and dot that is in use.

  If fewer than two history points exist, the panel prints:
  "Not Enough History Data".

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  8.3  PANEL: CURRENT ACTIVE TRADES
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Title bar note: pager, or the count of open positions.

  Live positions, refreshed continuously. Columns:

    SYMBOL ........ The instrument.
    TYPE .......... BUY in green, SELL in red.
    VOLUME ........ Lots.
    OPEN .......... Open price, at the instrument's own digit count.
    CURRENT ....... Current price.
    PIPS / MOVE ... For a Forex pair, the move in pips, in the direction of
                    the trade, to one decimal. For a non-Forex instrument such
                    as gold, an index or a crypto pair, the RAW PRICE MOVE to
                    two decimals, because "pips" has no agreed meaning on
                    those instruments. Section 16 explains this fully.
    SWAP .......... Swap accumulated on that position so far.
    PROFIT ........ The floating profit of the position, WITHOUT swap. Swap
                    is shown in its own column beside it.
    CLOSE ......... The two step close button, if it is switched on.

  The TOTAL line at the foot of the panel shows the total volume of all open
  positions and the total floating result. Note carefully: this total INCLUDES
  swap, while the PROFIT column of each row EXCLUDES it. The two figures will
  therefore differ by exactly the sum of the SWAP column. This is intentional
  - the row shows you the market result, the total shows you the true cash
  position.

  Beside the total line, a status line appears when something needs saying:
  the close confirmation prompt with its countdown, or the result of the last
  close attempt. Result messages disappear on their own after eight seconds.

  When there is nothing open, the panel prints "No Open Trades".


===============================================================================
  SECTION 09  -  TAB 2  -  STATISTICS
===============================================================================

  The report card of the account. Three panels.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  9.1  PANEL: TRADE STATISTICS
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Title bar note: the number of closed trades. If the account has traded any
  non-Forex instrument, the note adds, for example:

      "184 Closed Trades   |   17 Non-FX (Excluded From Pips)"

  That second half is a deliberate honesty note. It tells you exactly how many
  trades were left out of every pip figure on the screen.

  Eighteen values in three columns of six rows.

    COLUMN 1

    Trades ............ Number of fully closed trades.
    Profitability ..... A green and red bar, with the win rate printed to its
                        left. The green part is the share of winning trades.
    Won / Lost ........ Counts of winners and losers.
    Pips (FX) ......... Total pips across Forex trades only. Shows "n/a" when
                        the account has never traded a Forex pair.
    Lots .............. Total volume opened.
    Commissions ....... Total commission paid.

    COLUMN 2

    Average Win ....... Average pips and average money of a winning trade.
    Average Loss ...... Average pips and average money of a losing trade.
    Longs Won ......... (winners / total longs) and the percentage.
    Shorts Won ........ (winners / total shorts) and the percentage.
    Profit Factor ..... Gross profit divided by gross loss. Green at 1.00 or
                        above, red below.
    Expectancy ........ Net profit divided by the number of closed trades. In
                        plain words: what one average trade is worth to this
                        account, in money.

    COLUMN 3

    Best Trade ........ Date and money of the single largest winner.
    Worst Trade ....... Date and money of the single largest loser.
    Best (Pips) ....... Date and pips of the largest Forex winner.
    Worst (Pips) ...... Date and pips of the largest Forex loser.
    Avg. Trade Length . Average time a trade was held.
    Total Swap ........ Total swap over all closed trades.

  Teaching note. Profit Factor and Win Rate should always be read together.
  A win rate of 80% with a profit factor of 0.8 means the account wins often
  and loses badly - the classic profile of an account that will eventually be
  wiped out by one trade. A win rate of 35% with a profit factor of 2.1 is a
  healthy trend following account.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  9.2  PANEL: MONTHLY GAIN (CHANGE)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Title bar note: the total for the selected year, in percent and in money.

  A vertical bar for each month of the selected year. Green above the zero
  line, red below it. The percentage is printed at the tip of each bar and the
  month name under the axis.

  Each month's percentage is that month's trading profit divided by the
  account balance at the START of that month. This is the standard monthly
  gain used by public trading statistics sites, and it is the reason a month
  that made the same money as another month can show a different percentage -
  the account was a different size at the time.

  Click a year on the strip of year chips at the top of the panel to move
  between years. If the account has no monthly data at all, the panel prints
  "No Monthly Data".

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  9.3  PANEL: CURRENCY POPULARITY
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A donut chart on the left, a paged table on the right.

  The donut divides the account by TRADE COUNT, not by profit. The number in
  the centre is the total number of closed trades. Each slice has its own
  colour, and the same colour is repeated as a small square beside the name in
  the table, so the eye can match them.

  The table has four columns:

    SYMBOL / STRATEGY .. The instrument name, or the strategy name.
    SHARE .............. That entry's share of the trade count, in percent.
    NET ................ Net money made or lost on that entry.
    R:R ................ Average win divided by average loss for that entry.

  SECTION 13  -  ONE CLICK TRADE CLOSING, EXPLAINED IN FULL
===============================================================================

  This is the only feature in the product that can change your money. Read
  this section completely before you use it, and make sure every student has
  read it too.

  13.1  WHERE IT IS
  -----------------
  Overview tab  >  Current Active Trades  >  the CLOSE column on the far right
  of each row.

  13.2  HOW TO CLOSE A POSITION
  -----------------------------
  It is deliberately a TWO STEP action.

    STEP 1.  Click the red "X" on the row of the position you want to close.

             The button changes to "OK" and turns into a filled red chip. A
             status line appears at the foot of the panel:

                 "Click OK Again To Close Ticket 123456789   (6s)"

             The number in brackets counts down every second.

    STEP 2.  Click "OK" before the countdown reaches zero.

             The position is closed at market. The status line is replaced by
             the result, for example:

                 "Closed EURUSD  Ticket 123456789"

  If you do NOT click the second time, the arming expires on its own and the
  button returns to a plain "X". Nothing is closed. A forgotten first click
  cannot sit waiting to fire later.

  You can also cancel deliberately: click the "X" of a DIFFERENT row, or
  switch the theme. Either action clears the armed ticket.

  13.3  WHY TWO STEPS
  -------------------
  The dashboard covers the whole chart. A single stray click, a slipping
  mouse, or a student demonstrating something on a live account could
  otherwise close a real position instantly and irreversibly. One click arms;
  a second, conscious click executes. Nothing closes by accident.

  13.4  THE COUNTDOWN CLOCK
  -------------------------
  The countdown uses the computer's own millisecond clock, not the broker's
  server time. This matters: server time only advances when a price tick
  arrives, so on a quiet symbol or over a weekend the countdown would have
  frozen and an armed ticket would never have expired. The computer clock
  keeps running whether or not the market does.

  13.5  SETTINGS THAT CONTROL IT
  ------------------------------
    Show One Click Close Button On Open Trades .... default true
        Set this to false and the CLOSE column disappears completely. The
        dashboard becomes strictly read only. This is the recommended setting
        for a shared classroom terminal or a student demonstration account.

    Confirm Window Seconds (Click X Twice) ........ default 6
        How long the armed state lasts. Whatever you type, the working value
        is forced into the range 2 to 60 seconds. A value of zero would have
        thrown away the entire safety guard, so it is not permitted.

    Maximum Slippage In Points .................... default 30
        The maximum price movement, in points, that you accept between your
        click and the fill. If the market moves further than this in that
        instant, the broker rejects the close and the dashboard reports the
        rejection rather than filling you at a worse price.

  13.6  MESSAGES YOU MAY SEE, AND WHAT THEY MEAN
  ----------------------------------------------
  Every message appears on the status line at the foot of the Current Active
  Trades panel and clears itself after about eight seconds.

    "AutoTrading Is Switched Off In The Terminal Toolbar"
        The AutoTrading button on the main toolbar is red. Click it once.

        Press F7 on the chart, open the Common tab, tick "Allow Algo Trading",

    "This Account Does Not Allow Expert Advisor Trading"

===============================================================================
  SECTION 15  -  GLOSSARY: THE MEANING AND THE FORMULA OF EVERY NUMBER
===============================================================================

  This section is written so that it can be handed to a student on its own.
  Each entry gives the plain meaning first and the exact formula second.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ABSOLUTE GAIN  (Abs. Gain)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : How much trading has added, measured against the money you
                  actually put in.
  Formula ....... Net closed profit  /  total deposits  x  100

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  GAIN  (Time Weighted)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : Growth from TRADING only, with deposits and withdrawals
                  removed from the picture.
  How it works .. The history is cut into segments at every deposit and every
                  withdrawal. The growth factor of each segment is calculated
                  separately, and the segments are then multiplied together.
                  A deposit therefore starts a new segment instead of
                  registering as a gain.
  Why it matters  Without this, any account can show enormous "growth" simply
                  by receiving more money. This figure cannot be faked that
                  way. It is the same method used by professional fund
                  reporting.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DAILY %  and  MONTHLY %
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The steady compounded rate that would have produced the same
                  overall growth over the same period.
  Formula ....... days = calendar days from the first record to the last
                  Daily   = ( growth factor ^ (1 / days)  - 1 ) x 100
                  Monthly = ( growth factor ^ (30 / days) - 1 ) x 100
  Warning ....... These are averages spread over the whole history. They are
                  NOT a forecast and NOT the return of today or this month.
                  A student who multiplies the monthly figure by twelve to
                  "plan" a year has misunderstood it.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MAXIMUM DRAWDOWN
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The deepest fall the account has ever taken from its own
                  highest point.
  Formula ....... At every point: ( peak balance - current balance ) / peak
                  balance x 100. The maximum value ever reached is kept, in
                  both percent and money.
  Note .......... This is a BALANCE drawdown, computed from closed deals. It
                  does not include floating loss on positions that were still
                  open. See Section 16.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WIN RATE
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The share of closed trades that did not lose money.
  Formula ....... winning trades / total closed trades x 100
  Note .......... A trade that closed at exactly zero is counted as a WIN, not
                  as a loss. This is a deliberate convention. On accounts with
                  many breakeven exits it makes the win rate slightly kinder
                  than a strict "profit greater than zero" definition would.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PROFIT FACTOR
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : How many rupees the account earned for every rupee it lost.
  Formula ....... gross profit of all winners / absolute gross loss of all
                  losers
  Reading it .... Below 1.00 the account is losing. 1.00 is breakeven. Above
                  about 1.30 is respectable for a retail account. Above 3.00
                  on a small sample usually means the sample is too small, not
                  that the strategy is exceptional.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  EXPECTANCY
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : What one average trade is worth, in money.
  Formula ....... net profit / number of closed trades
  Reading it .... Multiply it by your expected number of trades per month to
                  get an honest, arithmetic expectation. If expectancy is
                  negative, no amount of trading more often can help.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  REWARD : RISK  (per symbol / per strategy)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : When this instrument pays, how much bigger is the payment
                  than the average loss it takes?
  Formula ....... ( gross win / number of winners )  divided by
                  ( absolute gross loss / number of losers )
  Note .......... Historical, not planned. If there are no losers yet, the
                  ratio does not exist and the panel says "No Loss".

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SHARPE RATIO (Annualised)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : Return per unit of variability. It asks whether the profit
                  arrived smoothly or through wild swings.
  How it works .. A daily return series is built. Each day's return is that
                  day's net trading result divided by the account balance at
                  the start of the day. From that series:

                    Sharpe = ( average daily return - daily risk free rate )
                             / standard deviation of daily returns
                             x  square root of 252

                  252 is the standard number of trading days in a year, used
                  to turn a daily figure into an annual one. The daily risk
                  free rate is your Risk Free Rate % setting divided by 100
                  and then by 252.

  Reading it .... Below 1 is weak. 1 to 2 is good. Above 2 is very good, and
                  should make you check the sample size before celebrating.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SORTINO RATIO (Annualised)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The same idea as Sharpe, but it only punishes DOWNSIDE
                  variability. Sharpe treats a violently profitable day as
                  "risk"; Sortino does not.
  How it works .. Identical to Sharpe, except that the divisor is built from
                  negative days only.
  Reading it .... Sortino is normally higher than Sharpe. If Sortino is much
                  higher, the account's variability is mostly on the winning
                  side, which is a good sign.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Z-SCORE
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : Do wins and losses come in streaks, or are they random?
  How it works .. The classic runs test. A "run" is an unbroken sequence of
                  wins or of losses. The test compares how many runs actually
                  occurred against how many would be expected by pure chance.

                    N = number of trades
                    W = number of wins,  L = number of losses,  R = runs
                    P = 2 x W x L
                    Z = ( N x (R - 0.5) - P ) / square root of
                        ( P x (P - N) / (N - 1) )

  Reading it .... Roughly, a value beyond +1.96 or -1.96 is statistically
                  meaningful at the usual 95% confidence level.
                  NEGATIVE means streaks: wins follow wins and losses follow
                  losses more often than chance. A trend following account
                  often looks like this.
                  POSITIVE means alternation: wins and losses take turns more
                  often than chance.
                  Near zero means the results look random, which is the normal
                  and expected result for most accounts.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  RECOVERY FACTOR
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : How much profit the account produced for each unit of pain
                  it went through at its worst moment.
  Formula ....... net profit / maximum drawdown in money
  Reading it .... Below 1 means the account has not yet earned back more than
                  its own worst fall. Above 3 is strong.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ACTIVE DAYS
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The number of days on which the account produced a non zero
                  trading result.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PIPS
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The standard unit of price movement for a currency pair.
  How it works .. On a 5 digit or 3 digit quote, one pip is ten points. On any
                  other quote, one pip is one point.
  Direction ..... For a SELL the sign is inverted, so a profitable sell shows
                  POSITIVE pips.
  Restriction ... Pips are calculated ONLY for instruments the broker
                  classifies as Forex. Everything else is excluded from every
                  pip total. See Section 16.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MONTHLY GAIN
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : What that month added, relative to the size of the account
                  at the start of that month.
  Formula ....... month profit / balance at start of month x 100

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MAXIMUM CONSECUTIVE LOSSES
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Plain meaning : The longest unbroken run of losing trades in the whole
                  history.
  Why it matters  This, and not the win rate, is the number that decides how
                  big a position you can safely take. If your history contains
                  a run of nine losses, your position size must survive ten.

  SECTION 16  -  HONEST LIMITS AND KNOWN BEHAVIOUR  (PLEASE READ)
===============================================================================

  Every analytics product has boundaries. Hiding them is how students end up
  drawing wrong conclusions from right numbers. These are ours, stated
  plainly.

  16.1  IT IS AN EXPERT ADVISOR, NOT AN INDICATOR
  -----------------------------------------------
  Earlier versions of Taurus Vision were custom indicators. MetaTrader 5 does
  not allow a custom indicator to call any trade function - an indicator that
  tries fails at runtime. Because Version 9.00 closes positions, it HAD to
  become an Expert Advisor.

  Two practical consequences:

    -  It lives in MQL5\Experts, not MQL5\Indicators.
    -  MetaTrader 5 permits only ONE Expert Advisor per chart. If you run a
       trading robot, give the dashboard its own chart.

  16.2  PIPS ARE CALCULATED FOR FOREX PAIRS ONLY
  ----------------------------------------------
  The word "pip" has an agreed meaning on a currency pair and no agreed
  meaning anywhere else. On a two digit crypto quote, a 46 dollar move becomes
  "4,674 pips", which is a meaningless number that would then pollute every
  pip average on the screen.

  Therefore:

    -  Only instruments the broker classifies as Forex contribute to Pips
       (FX), Average Win pips, Average Loss pips, Best (Pips) and Worst
       (Pips).
    -  Everything else - gold, silver, indices, crypto, CFDs on shares - is
       excluded from those figures.
    -  The Trade Statistics title bar tells you exactly how many trades were
       excluded, for example "17 Non-FX (Excluded From Pips)".
    -  In the trade tables the PIPS column still shows something useful for
       those instruments: the raw price movement, to two decimals.
    -  If the account has never traded a Forex pair, the pip fields show
       "n/a" rather than a misleading zero.

  16.3  SHARPE AND SORTINO ARE SLIGHTLY OPTIMISTIC
  ------------------------------------------------
  Both ratios are built from a series of daily returns, and that series only
  contains days on which the account actually had activity. Days with no
  trading at all are not in the series.

  Excluding flat days removes a set of zero returns that would otherwise pull
  the average down and change the standard deviation. The practical effect is
  that these two figures are somewhat HIGHER here than a strict calendar based
  Sharpe would be.

  This does not make them useless - comparing one period of your own account
  against another period of the same account is perfectly valid. But do not
  place our Sharpe beside a fund's published Sharpe and treat them as equals.

  16.4  DRAWDOWN IS BALANCE BASED
  -------------------------------
  The drawdown figures are computed from the closed deal history, so they
  measure the fall of the BALANCE. Floating loss on positions that were still
  open at the time is not included.

  An account that holds a very large losing position for weeks without closing
  it will therefore show a smaller drawdown here than its equity curve
  actually suffered.

  This is not an oversight, it is a limit of the data. MetaTrader 5 does not
  store a historical record of equity or of floating profit. Only a program
  that was RUNNING at the time, recording every tick, could reconstruct it.
  Nothing can recover it afterwards.

  16.5  THE HOUR CHART IS IN SERVER TIME
  --------------------------------------
  The Net P/L By Hour Of Day panel uses broker server hours, and says so in
  its own title bar. Check your broker's server offset before concluding
  anything about sessions.

  16.6  EVERYTHING DEPENDS ON DOWNLOADED HISTORY
  ----------------------------------------------
  The dashboard can only see the deals your terminal has actually received. A
  freshly installed terminal, or a terminal that has been asked to show only
  the last month, will show a shorter history. Set the Toolbox History tab to
  "All History" for the complete picture.

  16.7  BREAKEVEN TRADES COUNT AS WINS
  ------------------------------------
  A trade closing at exactly zero is counted as a winner. Section 15 explains
  this.

  16.8  THE PROFIT COLUMN AND THE TOTAL LINE DIFFER BY SWAP
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  In the Current Active Trades panel, the PROFIT column of each row shows the
  market result without swap, because swap has its own column. The TOTAL line
  at the bottom includes swap. The difference between them is exactly the sum
  of the SWAP column.

  16.9  WHAT THE PRODUCT CANNOT SHOW, AND WHY
  -------------------------------------------
  Two features that traders often ask for are simply not reconstructable from
  MetaTrader 5 deal history, no matter how the software is written:

    -  MAE and MFE excursion charts, which show how far a trade went against
       you and in your favour BEFORE it closed. These require tick by tick
       logging while the trade is open. History does not store it.

    -  A true historical EQUITY curve including floating profit. As explained
       in 16.4, MetaTrader 5 does not keep it.

  Any product claiming to produce these from history alone on an account it
  was not running on is estimating, not reporting.

  16.10  SETTINGS ARE NOT SAVED BETWEEN RESTARTS
  ----------------------------------------------
  The tab you are on, the pages you have scrolled to, the panels you have
  collapsed and the theme you have chosen live only while the dashboard runs.
  Restart it and it returns to the state described by your input settings.

  If you want a particular startup layout permanently, set it in the START-UP
  VIEW group and save the whole thing as a chart template.

  SECTION 17  -  TROUBLESHOOTING
===============================================================================

  PROBLEM  Nothing appears after I drag it onto the chart.
  CHECK    Look at the top right corner of the chart. A small sad face means
           the Expert Advisor is loaded but not permitted; a smiling face
           means it is running. If there is no face at all, the Expert Advisor
           did not attach - repeat Step 5 of Section 4. Also check the Experts
           tab in the Toolbox for a message.

  PROBLEM  The screen says "Chart Window Too Small".
  CHECK    Maximise the chart window inside the terminal. If it is already
           maximised, lower "Master Font Size %" from 115 to 95 or 85.

  PROBLEM  The header shows the product name and a red warning line.
  CHECK    Your name has not been set. Press F7, Inputs tab, first field.

  PROBLEM  The close buttons do nothing.
  CHECK    In order:
             1. Is the AutoTrading button on the toolbar green?
             2. Press F7, Common tab - is "Allow Algo Trading" ticked?
             3. Read the status line at the foot of the Current Active Trades
                panel. It names the exact problem. Section 13.6 lists every
                message.

  PROBLEM  There are no close buttons at all.
  CHECK    "Show One Click Close Button On Open Trades" is set to false, or
           there are no open positions.

  PROBLEM  The Growth panel says "Not Enough History Data".
  CHECK    The terminal has fewer than two records. Open Toolbox (Ctrl+T),
           History tab, right click, "All History". On a brand new account
           this message is simply correct.

  PROBLEM  Pips show "n/a", or the totals look too small.
  CHECK    Read Section 16.2. If this account trades only gold, indices or
           crypto, "n/a" is the correct and honest answer.

  PROBLEM  A pager arrow is missing.
  CHECK    The "<" arrow is dropped when the panel is too narrow to hold it.
           The ">" arrow wraps around from the last page to the first, so
           every page is still reachable. Widen the chart, or lower the font
           size, to bring the back arrow back.

  PROBLEM  Clicking sometimes seems to be ignored.
  CHECK    Two clicks on the same control within 300 milliseconds count as
           one. Slow down slightly between clicks.

  PROBLEM  The terminal feels slow on a very old account.
  CHECK    Raise "History Recalculation Interval (Sec)" from 5 to 15, or set
           "History Days" to 365 so that only the last year is studied.

  PROBLEM  My chart looks wrong after I remove the dashboard.
  CHECK    It should not. The background colour, price scale, time scale, one
           click panel and OHLC line are all saved on startup and restored on
           removal. If something remains changed, press F8 and reset the chart
           properties manually.

  PROBLEM  The numbers do not match my broker's statement exactly.
  CHECK    Some brokers report commission on a separate line, or apply it at a
           different moment. This dashboard includes commission and swap
           inside the net result of each trade. Compare a single trade in the
           Closed Trade History against the same trade in the terminal's own
           History tab; they will agree.

  Q.  Which chart and which timeframe should I use?
  A.  Any. The dashboard analyses the ACCOUNT, not the chart. Most traders
      keep one dedicated chart for it.

  Q.  Can I run it on more than one chart at the same time?
  A.  Yes, but there is no reason to. Every copy would show the same account.
      It also costs processing power for nothing.

  Q.  Can I run it alongside my trading robot?
  A.  Yes, but on a DIFFERENT chart. MetaTrader 5 allows only one Expert
      Advisor per chart.

  Q.  Is it safe on a live account?
  A.  It reads your history and it never opens or modifies a trade. The only
      action it can take is closing a position you clicked and then confirmed.
      For extra safety on a classroom or demonstration terminal, set "Show One
      Click Close Button On Open Trades" to false; the product then becomes
      strictly read only.

  Q.  Does it send my data anywhere?
  A.  No. It makes no internet connection of any kind.

  Q.  Does it need an internet licence or activation?
  A.  No.

  Q.  Does it work on a cent account or on a PKR account?
  A.  Yes. Set "Money Prefix" to blank and the real account currency code is
      used in front of every figure instead of a dollar sign.

  Q.  Does it work on a netting account?
  A.  Yes. Positions are reconstructed from deals using the position
      identifier, which works under both hedging and netting.

  Q.  Why does a partially closed position show one average price?
  A.  Because that is the honest summary. When a position was entered or
      exited in several parts, the IN and OUT prices are the volume weighted
      averages of those parts.

  Q.  Why is my win rate high but my profit negative?
  A.  Because win rate says nothing about SIZE. Look at Average Win against
      Average Loss, and at Profit Factor. This is the single most common
      pattern among losing retail accounts.

  Q.  Can I change the colours to my own brand?
  A.  Yes. Set "Use Custom Colours Below" to true and set the nine colours.
      Note that the theme switch stops changing the appearance while custom
      colours are active.

  A.  No. It is part of the product identity.

  Q.  Can I give this to my friend?

      not by this help file.

  Q.  Will there be more Taurus products?
  A.  Taurus Vision is one member of a planned family. Ask ForexGuru.Pk for
      the current list.

  19.1  A THIRTY MINUTE CLASSROOM SEQUENCE
  ----------------------------------------
  A sequence that works well with a live or demo account on a projector:

    1.  Switch to the LIGHT theme first. Dark gold on a projector washes out.
    2.  Overview tab. Set Account Growth to GROWTH % and point at the two
        lines. Ask the class which one is real growth and why the grey line
        can rise while the gold line stays flat.
    3.  Switch the same panel to DRAWDOWN. Ask how many WEEKS the account
        spent under water, not how deep it went. Depth frightens students;
        duration teaches them.
    4.  Statistics tab. Cover the Profit Factor with your hand and ask the
        class to predict it from the win rate alone. Then reveal it. This
        breaks the win rate obsession faster than any lecture.
    5.  Analytics tab. Average Holding Time. Ask why the green bar and the red
        bar differ. Let them find the "cutting winners, holding losers" bias
        themselves.
    6.  History tab. Risk Metrics & Records. Point at Max Cons. Losses and ask
        each student to calculate what their own risk per trade would have
        done through that run.

  19.2  MAKING GOOD SCREENSHOTS FOR STUDENTS
  ------------------------------------------
    -  Switch to the LIGHT theme. It prints and projects far better.
    -  Collapse every panel except the one you want to teach. That single
        panel then expands to fill the screen.
    -  Raise "Master Font Size %" to 140 or 150 for a projector.
    -  The footer already carries the trader name, currency and account
        number, so the picture identifies itself.

  19.3  A PRIVACY WARNING BEFORE YOU PUBLISH
  ------------------------------------------
  The header shows the broker name and the ACCOUNT LOGIN NUMBER, and the
  footer repeats the account number. Before you post a screenshot publicly or
  send it into a student group, cover both. Use a demo account for public
  material.

  19.4  A HOMEWORK EXERCISE THAT WORKS
  ------------------------------------
  Ask each student to send you three screenshots of their own account:

    1.  Statistics tab with History Days set to 0.
    2.  Statistics tab with History Days set to 90.
    3.  Analytics tab with History Days set to 90.

  Then ask them one question: "In the last ninety days, did you get better or
  did you get luckier?" The Profit Factor, the Expectancy and the Reward:Risk
  panel between them will answer it, and the student will have done the work.
